# 26.48.2

## Develop

- **Highlight reconstruction (Rebuild):** a partly clipped area with no reliable colour nearby now takes its own colour from just below the clip (as far as its unclipped channels agree) instead of a neutral guess. With green and blue clipped and red not, the neutral guess lifted green to blue's level, so large bright skies rendered cyan once Exposure or Highlights brought them below white (#1487).
- **Crop past the edge (#861):** Crop ▸ Extend Past the Edge lets the crop reach past the photo (up to half its size on each side), shown as blank canvas; Fill Edges fills it Content-Aware (on this computer) or Generative (your own generative fill service and key). Commands `crop.extend`, `crop.fillEdges`, `crop.clearFill`, `crop.cancelFill`.
- **Tool row customisation:** every button under the histogram but Edit (Browse Profiles and Compact's Reset included) can be hidden or moved from the row's context menu or Settings ▸ Develop ▸ Tool Row (#1251).
- **Detachable mask list:** an off-by-default setting puts the masks in a window of their own over the photo; Feather, Refine Edges and the mask's sliders stay in the panel (#1251).
- **Camera calibrations:** `calibrate-lightroom fit` measures, from a set with both converted DNGs and raws, the baseline exposure Lightroom gives the camera's raws (what a converted DNG carries as `BaselineExposure`), and writes it as the calibration's `raw_baseline_exposure`. A raw without colour tags of its own gets it under that calibration, so it renders as bright as its DNG (#1485).

## Library

- **Undo follows navigation:** a new source (folder, collection), filter, selection or module is a step of Undo and Redo, so Undo no longer changes a photo that is not on screen. Typing in the search box is one step per word.
- **Import → Copy, durable:** a copy counts as imported only once its folder entry is on disk (as Move already did); otherwise it is reported failed and removed.
- **Sidecar saves, every IPTC field:** Headline, Caption Writer, Category, Supplemental Categories, Subject Code, Country Code and plug-in metadata fields are replaced in an existing sidecar too; before, only a new sidecar got them, and the next read restored the old value.
- **Advanced Search dates and more:** capture, import and edit date spans, capture month and day of the month (any year), pick flag, file size, exposure bias and flash. Smart-collection rules gain Capture Month, Capture Day and File Size (MB) (#1135).
- **Advanced Search groups:** a search's conditions can match all, any, none or not all, and search groups with their own choice combine as all or any, as in digiKam (#1135).
- **Advanced Search lists:** file format, camera, lens, keyword and people are picked from the library's own values, and photos without keywords, a camera or a lens can be found (#1135).
- **Map follows the filmstrip:** one click on a filmstrip thumbnail centres the map on that photo at the zoom it has, as in Lightroom Classic. The map glides there when the pin is within three map widths and jumps when it is farther; a photo without a position leaves the map alone, and a drag, zoom or search ends a glide. Before, the map only moved when the pin was off screen, or on a double-click (which still frames the pin and zooms in).
- **GPS badge:** grid cells of photos with a GPS position show a map marker with their other thumbnail badges (click: the Map, on that photo), and filmstrip thumbnails show it with their marks, as in Lightroom Classic. View Options ▸ Show Thumbnail Badges and the filmstrip badges setting hide it.
- **Saving large steps:** a step such as a Lightroom catalog import is written to the library's log in records of about 4 MiB, not one record of gigabytes; saving a synthetic 200,000-photo import peaked at 7.9 GB instead of 9.0 GB (#1361).
- **Lightroom import:** no more one-million-row limit per table; the read keeps within a memory budget taken from the computer (`<PREFIX>_LIGHTROOM_IMPORT_MEMORY` to change it), never skips a table for lack of memory, and keeps about half what it did (#1361).
- Lightroom catalog imports are committed and saved in parts, still one undo step, so a large catalog needs less memory (#1361).
- **Map pin card:** hovering a pin opens a card as in Lightroom Classic: the photo, its file name, capture time and exposure, and for a pin of several photos "1 of N" with arrows that step through them (round the ends). The card stays while the pointer moves onto it; a click on the photo selects it. It replaces the thumbnail-and-count preview.
- **Map search and saved locations:** the search box matches saved locations ignoring accents as well as case, however the name stores them ("avila" and "Ávila" now find names with a decomposed Á, as some imported catalogs have). When several match, they are listed under the box to pick from instead of the map jumping to the first; a name typed in full still goes straight there. With online lookups on, the list ends with **Search online for “…”**, so a place named like a saved location can still be looked up (`map.search {online: true}` does the same). `map.search` returns `{found: "savedLocations", names}` for several.
- **More Metadata columns, and Face Count:** the filter bar shows as many Metadata columns as you like, added, moved and removed from a column's header, and a new Face Count column narrows by how many faces a photo has (#1112).
- **Reload from Disk reports unread files:** files it can't read (gone, unreadable, on a drive that isn't responding) are counted in its message instead of being skipped silently; `photo.reload` returns them as `failed: [{id, error}]`.
- **Saved Locations list:** each location's box now works as in Lightroom Classic: ticked when the selected photos are inside the location, mixed when some are, and ticking it moves the selected photos to its centre (one undo step). It used to switch Private, which stays in the Edit Location panel; private locations show 🔒. `map.locations` reports `selected` for each location. Dropping photos on a row no longer shows a toast as well as the overlay.
- **Find Duplicates, one group at a time:** each group can be settled as soon as it is reviewed (Remove N and Keep the Rest, Keep All), or every group down to it at once (↑ Apply This and All Above), instead of the whole list in one go (#1399).

## Import, export and formats

- **Pixel Shift merge registers the frames:** Photo ▸ Photo Merge ▸ Pixel Shift measures the 16 frames' positions on the data (tiles of the green samples, Lucas–Kanade fits against a reference built from the other frames, a similarity model per frame, a hold-out test against the camera's own pattern, brightness gains for flickering light) and reconstructs the image by local kernel regression, instead of placing the frames on the camera's table. A series that cannot be measured or measures worse than the table is merged plainly with the reason in a message. **Pixel Shift, Plain Merge** (`merge.pixelShift method=plain`, `<cli> merge pixel-shift --plain`) keeps the earlier merge. The merged file's XMP and the catalog record the method, the hold-out verdict, why a plain merge was used and whether gains were applied (catalog format 22); the XMP also keeps each frame's model. Progress names the stage and cancelling leaves nothing behind. About 55 to 95 s on 24 threads and 85 to 125 s on 8 threads, plus about 4 s to write the file, and 2.6 GB for a 45-megapixel Z 8 series; the app hints from ISO 1600. Even on a steady tripod the frames' measured image positions don't match the camera's table (horizontal step about 0.7 to 1.0 pixel instead of 1), so registration helps almost every series. **Changed:** a merge of a series that already has one is no longer refused; the first version refused when `<name>-PixelShift.dng` was there. The registered result keeps that name, the plain one is `<name>-PixelShift-Plain.dng`, a taken name gets `-2`, `-3`…, nothing is replaced, and every result is imported and stacked with its own provenance (including the time it was made). Uses the `rustfft` crate (MIT or Apache-2.0). Method and limits: `docs/pixel-shift.md`.
- **Pixel Shift merges are read safely from files that someone may have crafted:** the provenance block of a merged DNG and the maker notes of the frames are bounded (packet size, shot count, frame numbers, string lengths, the sensor window, and the per-frame models of a registered merge, the tile count and the time it was made) and a block that breaks a limit is no block: the DNG opens as the normal DNG it is. A value a newer version may write (a method, a verdict, a reason or an engine number this version does not know) reads as unknown and never makes a file or the library unreadable. When a merged DNG is imported on its own, its frames are found in the library again only when everything agrees: same folder, file name and capture time, the frame's own maker note giving the listed frame number and exposure counter, and all the frames together being one series; otherwise none is found and **Delete Merge Sources** has nothing to act on, so a crafted DNG can no longer send unrelated photos to Recently Deleted. Several merges of one stack that list the same frames are each checked on their own; after the first Delete Merge Sources the others find the frames missing and touch nothing. Seeded mutation tests of both readers run in the test suite (`LCC_FUZZ_ITERS` makes them longer). Rules and limits: `docs/pixel-shift.md`.

## Import

- **Each drive finds its own number of readers:** the I/O pool learns a limit per drive from its throughput and the whole process's (one drive tries a change at a time, judged against fresh measurements before and after), starting from the previous thread count and capped at 32 reads at once across all drives; a drive that several cards are copied into keeps at least two copies at once; a drive with no files for 10 minutes is forgotten. The import's read-ahead per drive grows with its limit.
- **A hung drive during Copy or Move:** copies run per drive (each copy holds a place on its card and on its destination, starting with as many copies as before, then each drive finding its own number), so a card or destination that stops answering fails only its own files — "volume not responding", or "outcome unknown" for a copy that was under way, which may still appear in the destination folder outside the library; the original is always kept. Cancel returns even while a drive hangs; a raw waiting for Copy as DNG when you cancel keeps its raw copy.
- **Lightroom catalogs, captions and stacks:** caption rows whose image isn't a whole photo id, and stack rows whose stack isn't a whole stack id, are skipped with a warning, as other tables' rows already were; before, image 1.5 gave photo 1 its caption and rows with no stack id were all stacked together.
- **Lightroom catalogs, "not set" values:** Lightroom's not-set marker (-999999) in a photo's cached settings is left out before the settings are read, so an older process version's Shadows no longer comes in as Blacks +100.
- **Several drives at once:** the import scan, Synchronize Folder and the command-line Lightroom import read files per drive, side by side, each drive starting with as many readers as before (then finding its own number: see "Each drive finds its own number of readers"). A drive that stops answering has its files reported as "volume not responding" while the others go on, Cancel returns even while a drive hangs, and a file that crashes the reader fails alone.
- **Reads on one drive take turns:** every file read the scheduler runs goes through one pool for the app, so work reading the same drive at the same time (Reload, Find Missing or an import review beside Reading originals) shares that drive's readers and takes turns, instead of each adding its own; a few files are no longer held up behind thousands.
- **Several cards copied at once:** an import's reads and copies from several cards or drives run side by side through the I/O pool, each card read at most two batches ahead of its copies (plus two files for every reader its drive's limit has gained); planning keeps the file order, so names, rename sequence numbers, duplicate decisions and the batches the photos join the library in are unchanged. Reading originals reads the next batches' files ahead while it applies one.
- **Cancel lets a copy under way finish:** a copy still flushing its file to disk, reading it back on the destination, or converting it to DNG is no longer left behind 5 s after Cancel as "outcome unknown" (a file could then appear outside the library). Cancel's 5 s rule now counts silence as the hung-drive rule does: such work holds it for up to ten times the drive's stall time, then 5 s more without progress leaves the copy behind.
- **An import says when its file reader stopped:** files the I/O pool never started because its scheduler stopped (an internal error) are counted as "not imported: the app's file reader stopped; import them again" instead of "not readable"; `library.import` lists them in `interrupted` with `why`: `schedulerStopped` (besides `failed`).
- **An import says why files didn't come in:** files a drive that stopped responding kept out are counted as "not imported: a drive stopped responding", and copies left unfinished (the drive stopped, or Cancel, while they were under way) on their own with the folder where a file may still appear outside the library, instead of all being "not readable"; a batch the library couldn't add is reported as not added, with the reason. `library.import` reports these files in `interrupted` (`why`: `volumeNotResponding` or `outcomeUnknown`, with `volume` and `folder`) besides `failed`. With Copy as DNG on, a raw that wasn't converted is listed in Import Warnings by reason (codes `dngCancelled`, `dngMemoryHeld`, `dngFailed`); it was imported as its raw copy.
- **Large Lightroom catalogs keep their history:** the develop-history budget grows with the catalog's size and the computer's memory and is spent on every photo's newest steps first, so photos late in the table no longer lose theirs; the recovery archive may take up to a quarter of the drive's free space instead of being skipped past 32 MiB (#1312). Thanks @blwfish for the measurements.
- **Lightroom imports say which sidecars disagree:** imported photos start in step with their XMP sidecars, or as a metadata conflict (counted in the result) where a sidecar disagrees with the catalog, instead of all showing "changed in the catalog" (#1312). Thanks @blwfish for the measurements.

## Performance

- **Originals are read once after a catalog import:** reading the originals of a Lightroom catalog import (and
  Reload from Disk, the preview-only recheck and relinking a photo) now records the SHA-1 the same read computes, and
  the background checksum pass leaves photos still to be read to that read, so it no longer reads every file a
  second time; Immich lookups and device sync get the checksum as soon as the file is read.
- Automatic export lanes follow the GPU's measured share of the work: one fewer per stretch of photos while it is at least 90 % busy, one more while it is under 70 %, between two and the automatic count (#1377). Lanes set by hand or by Test my machine are kept as they are.
- Full-size Bayer raws demosaic (AHD) on the GPU, matched against the CPU's demosaic; X-Trans and machines without a GPU keep the CPU (#1377). `<PREFIX>_GPU_DEMOSAIC=0` turns it off.
- **ONNX Runtime on the graphics card (#491):** desktop downloads ship ONNX Runtime 1.32 and its WebGPU plug-in (Microsoft's builds, MIT; Dawn inside, BSD). AI Denoise and AI Super Resolution try it first on the card AI work goes to, checked against the CPU on a test tile, before the built-in wgpu code and the CPU. Without the library (the web, Android, riscv64, Intel Macs, or `<PREFIX>_ONNXRUNTIME=off`) nothing changes. `ml.status` reports `onnxRuntime`.

## Fixes

- **Reload from Disk and relinking:** a Reload that finds only the checksum new no longer adds an undo step (which
  cleared the redo history), and an undo no longer drops the checksum of the file on disk; Reload leaves Immich
  link-only photos alone (it re-read the server's preview as if it were the original); relinking a virtual copy or
  its master to another file no longer gives the rest of the family that file's size and content hash; reading
  originals fills in an empty camera serial number and lens range; undoing a step that recorded a checksum keeps the
  photo's device-sync record with it.
- **Start-up hang (Windows, Vulkan):** with an implicit Vulkan layer installed (RivaTuner, OBS's capture hook), some starts deadlocked: the window's swapchain set-up on the UI thread and the GPU renderer's device creation on a background thread each waited on the other inside the Vulkan loader. The renderer's device is now made only after the window has drawn its first frames.
- **Windows updates:** installing an update on an MSI-installed copy quit the app without running the installer, so the old version came back (#1549). The installer now runs, the app waits for it and restarts only when it succeeded; a failed or declined install is logged at the next start, with the installer's log kept beside the download. The always-greyed **Help ▸ Install Update and Relaunch** item was removed; the Updates dialog's button installs.

## Development

- **Diagnostics recorder:** off unless `<PREFIX>_DIAG` is set; records an import's probe, commit and library saves in an in-flight file, a bounded ring and per-item timings in the settings folder's `diag/`, so a hang or a kill leaves a record of what was running and a clean quit is marked as one. Nothing is sent anywhere (#1363).

## People

- People: the Unnamed and Suggested faces and a person's suggested faces follow a folder or collection picked in the sidebar, not only a date (#685).

## Export

- **Faster mid-size raw exports:** a raw exported at up to about half its sensor's size is binned two by two and resized once to the output instead of demosaiced whole; a 3,000 px JPEG from 45 MP takes about half the time and memory. Exports of 2,560 px and smaller lose an extra box resize and come out sharper (#1377).
- **Lossy WebP:** WebP exports are lossy at the chosen quality on desktop builds, with a **Lossless** box for exact copies (libwebp, BSD-3-Clause). Older presets now export lossy; the web and Android apps stay lossless.

## Raw processing

- **Raw decoder per camera:** Settings ▸ Import ▸ Raw decoder chooses Auto, the built-in decoder (lcc-raw) or LibRaw for each camera; `library.preferences` takes `rawDecoder: {camera, decoder}` and reports `rawDecoders` and the `libraw` build. Auto reads with the built-in decoder and lets LibRaw read the variants it can't; LibRaw falls back to the built-in decoder when it fails. Demosaic and development stay in the app. LibRaw is linked into desktop builds made with `LIBRAW_DIR` (or `LIBRAW_SYSTEM=1`); the web and Android builds have none and grey the choice out.
