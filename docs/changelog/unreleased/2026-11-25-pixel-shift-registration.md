## Import, export and formats

- **Pixel Shift merge registers the frames:** Photo ▸ Photo Merge ▸ Pixel Shift measures the 16 frames' positions on the data (tiles of the green samples, Lucas–Kanade fits against a reference built from the other frames, a similarity model per frame, a hold-out test against the camera's own pattern, brightness gains for flickering light) and reconstructs the image by local kernel regression, instead of placing the frames on the camera's table. A series that cannot be measured or measures worse than the table is merged plainly with the reason in a message. **Pixel Shift, Plain Merge** (`merge.pixelShift method=plain`, `<cli> merge pixel-shift --plain`) keeps the earlier merge. The merged file's XMP and the catalog record the method, the hold-out verdict, why a plain merge was used and whether gains were applied (catalog format 22); the XMP also keeps each frame's model. Progress names the stage and cancelling leaves nothing behind. About 55 to 95 s on 24 threads and 85 to 125 s on 8 threads, plus about 4 s to write the file, and 2.6 GB for a 45-megapixel Z 8 series; the app hints from ISO 1600. Even on a steady tripod the frames' measured image positions don't match the camera's table (horizontal step about 0.7 to 1.0 pixel instead of 1), so registration helps almost every series. **Changed:** a merge of a series that already has one is no longer refused; the first version refused when `<name>-PixelShift.dng` was there. The registered result keeps that name, the plain one is `<name>-PixelShift-Plain.dng`, a taken name gets `-2`, `-3`…, nothing is replaced, and every result is imported and stacked with its own provenance (including the time it was made). Uses the `rustfft` crate (MIT or Apache-2.0). Method and limits: `docs/pixel-shift.md`.
