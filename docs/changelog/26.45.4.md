# 26.45.4

## Develop and masking

- **The Masks list as a tree:** components hang under the selected mask as tree branches with their operation (+ − ∩) in a column of its own and an Invert button each, named after what they select; other masks' branches open with the chevron; the eye (the mask's effect on / off) stays on every row; an empty name restores the automatic one.

## Develop

- **Camera profiles made from a colour chart keep the photo's contrast:** a camera profile (`.dcp`) without a tone curve of its own (most chart-made ones: a colour matrix, maybe a hue/saturation map or look table) no longer renders the raw with the flat default tone. It keeps the tone the photo has without the profile: the file's own DNG tone curve, else the tone fitted to its camera JPEG, so the raw render keeps the contrast of the camera preview shown first. The colour still comes from the profile, and a profile with its own tone curve renders with it as before. This applies however the photo gets the profile (chosen per photo, an import default, a camera body's profile, the camera's default), in the grid, the loupe and exports; thumbnails, previews and smart previews made before are rebuilt. `photo.sourceReport` reports such a tone as `withoutProfile`.
- **Process version 8, smooth gradients, highlights and colour noise reduction:** 8-bit output (the view and 8-bit exports) is dithered with an 8×8 ordered pattern, so skies and vignettes no longer band; black, white and exact levels stay exact, and the CPU and GPU dither alike. Raw highlights also roll off into white in stops, so raising Exposure keeps what the sensor recorded instead of clipping it. Colour noise reduction filters the linear colour differences jointly, with edge-aware weights from a denoised guide and a brake for isolated colour specks: blotches and specks go at the default Color 25 and colour edges stay at 100; a zoomed-in view is filtered like the export, on the CPU and the GPU alike. Photos on earlier processes keep their look until Update to Current Process.

## Library

- **Import a library in the earlier catalog format:** a new File ▸ More Imports command reads a library in the catalog format this app had under its earlier name (format v10 and older, still written by the fork of that name) and merges its photos, edits, masks, picks, ratings, labels and collections through the Import from Catalog preview. The records are converted as they are read; the library itself is only read, never locked, upgraded or changed. A mask of a kind this app doesn't know is left out, with the rest of the photo's edits kept. The command and the format names live in the `legacy` files (`crates/catalog/src/legacy.rs`, `crates/ui-egui/src/legacy.rs`).
- **Auto Sync on the bottom bar, Show Before, Favorites:** the Compact bottom bar has an Auto Sync switch; `\` is named Show Before; the folders to browse are called Favorites.
- **Presets from an older install:** the shared presets take an older install's presets (develop presets with their marks, local adjustment presets, export presets) the first time a library opens, once; File ▸ More Imports ▸ Import Presets from Older Installs asks again.
- **Camera profiles from an older install:** Import Presets from Older Installs (and the first library open) also installs the older install's camera profiles (.dcp) the way Import Profiles & Presets does, with the camera bodies it started with them; one there already is skipped and a damaged one is named.

## Performance

- **Both graphics cards share batch work:** exports, Build Previews and the background preparation take a turn per photo on whichever card would finish it soonest (each card's speed is measured), instead of sending every batch render to the second card; the card for editing takes batch photos only while the interface is idle, one at a time, and a running one pauses between submissions when you touch a slider. 1:1 previews build two photos per card. Ported from OneSlips' dual-GPU work.
- **Culling shows your edit at once:** an edited photo never stands in with its unedited, uncropped camera JPEG; its latest rendered picture (kept in the preview cache, the last to be pruned) stands in, placed by its crop when its look is close, else a fast draft of the edit from the camera JPEG. The loupe makes the next photos' stand-ins and thumbnails ahead of the step, holds back the raw render while an arrow key is held (`perf.loupeSteps` in `ui.inspect`), shows a raw's full-size camera JPEG at 100 % until the window render arrives, and labels the camera JPEG as "Camera preview". New `bench-cull FOLDER` CLI command and `tools/cull-bench.py` time culling a real shoot.
- **AI Denoise on both graphics cards:** with a second graphics card, the built-in denoiser and installed models keep a device on each card, and a batch makes one photo on each card at once (the second card first; one photo at a time while you work). Each card is checked against the processor and given up on alone; a tile for the card for editing waits while you touch the interface. `denoise.status` lists both cards (`device.cards`) and both photos (`running.photos`), and each photo has its row under the AI Denoise batch row. Ported from OneSlips' dual-GPU work.
- **Graphics cards for exports:** with a second graphics card, Settings ▸ Performance ▸ Exports chooses which cards exports use: both (the default, each photo on the card that will finish it sooner), the card for editing only (the second card stays free for previews and thumbnails), or the second card only, to measure its speed (the card for editing takes over if it stops). `app.exportTuning {cards: both | editing | second}` sets it too. Ported from OneSlips' dual-GPU work.
- **Face recognition and two graphics cards:** the face recognition model keeps a device on each graphics card. It runs on the second graphics card when there is one (the card for editing stays free for the window), else on the card for editing; when its card stops working mid-scan, the rest of the scan moves to the other card, then to the processor. At a normal or full pace the second card is not held back, since it draws no window; while you work both are held back as before. `faces.gpu` names the card (`recognition.card`). Ported from OneSlips' dual-GPU work.

## Compact layout

- **Flags, overlay and shot info:** the Compact strip marks a pick with a solid flag and a reject with the same flag and a bold cross; the Masking panel's overlay switch is a button with its own icon, and the photo says "Overlay on · O" while it is on (not while a slider adjusts); choosing how the overlay looks no longer turns it on for good. The shot info adds a zoom lens's range when its name lacks it, the Import bar shows the browsed folder's parents as a breadcrumb, and the Upright caption matches the slider names.
- **AI-mask batch rows count photos:** an AI-mask batch row reads "1 of 3" while its first photo is at work, and says what it waits for ("Waiting for other AI masks") before it starts.

## Plug-ins

- Pixel filters may carry their own icon and header picture (manifest `icon` and `header`, SVG; `plugin.picture` gives them): the Plug-in Manager and Develop ▸ Plug-in Filters show them in place of the identicon and the name.
