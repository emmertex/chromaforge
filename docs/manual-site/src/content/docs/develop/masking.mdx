<Steps>

1. **Open the Masks tool.** The panel offers every way to make a mask.

   <Shot name="mask-1-open" alt="The Masks tool open, Create New Mask choices" />

2. **Pick a type and mark the area.** Here: **Linear**, then drag on the photo from the top edge down to where the effect should fade out.

   <Shot name="mask-2-linear" alt="A linear mask dragged on the sky" />

3. **Adjust the mask.** Its own Light, Colour, Effects and Detail sliders only touch the masked area. Colour's **Hue** is an angle in degrees: about ±34° (Lightroom's range), or ±180° with **Settings ▸ Interface ▸ Expanded sliders** on.

   <Shot name="mask-3-adjust" alt="Exposure, temperature, saturation and dehaze changed for the sky mask" />

4. **Add another mask.** Pick **Radial** and drag out from the centre of the sun. Each mask is listed in the panel.

   <Shot name="mask-4-radial" alt="A radial mask around the sun" />

5. **Leave the tool** to see the result without the overlay. After a brush stroke the overlay stays on until a mask slider moves.

   <Shot name="mask-5-result" alt="The finished photo with both masks applied" />

</Steps>

- **Delete**, **X** and the tool keys act only on a tool that is on screen, not on a saved panel in the grid, Compare, Survey or full-screen preview.

- **Create from the photo**: Object, Describe (text prompt), Subject, Sky, Background, People, Remembered (reuse a saved selection). AI selections need their model installed.
- **Draw**: Brush, Linear, Radial.
- **Range**: Luminance, Color.
- Add, subtract and intersect to refine; **Refine Edges** and **Feather** soften the border.
- Masks use image-relative coordinates, so previews and exports match at any size.

<Aside type="note" title="Modern layout">
  **Masks** is in the dock. Show or hide the mask overlay: **View ▸ Tool Overlay**. Brush size and feather: **Window ▸ Tools**.
</Aside>
